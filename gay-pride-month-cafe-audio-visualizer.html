⚙️ Radio Options & Tools

How radio works here

Each card plays that station's own actual live stream — the same streams behind the stations' own websites, linked on every card, and every feed was re-checked live on 2026-11-12, and 107.5 Frank FM's feed was checked live when it joined the cafe on 2026-11-13. Air1 and GayFM each have a second official live feed standing by (Air1's is EMF's; GayFM's is its second silvacast host, the one its own stream.gayfm.de address points to): if a main feed drops twice in a row, the player switches to that station's backup feed on its own. The player treats repeated stop-and-start stuttering on any station as a drop, so a stuttering station rejoins fresh or moves to its other official feed instead of buffering on and off forever — and for Air1 and GayFM the switch runs both ways, back to the main feed if the backup is the one that keeps failing. The visualizers dance to each station's exact beat: the seven stations' streams are open (CORS-enabled) feeds — re-checked live on 2026-11-13, Frank FM and GayFM's backup included — so each station's actual sound flows straight into the visualizer's Exact Beat engine (on by default in Beat & Levels), which measures the real sound fresh every frame with a fine 4096-point analysis, fires on the kick the instant it lands, counts the song's true tempo from the beats themselves — watch it in the Detected tempo readout — and drives the bars, beat meters, and Auto-DJ from that, for every station and for the Music Player alike. On 2026-11-14 the lock was tightened further for every station: the analysis smoothing was sharpened so a kick reaches the beat engine within a frame or two of the sound itself instead of trailing several frames behind it, the beat tracker now forgets the previous station's tempo the moment you hop stations (or a station switches to its backup feed) and re-locks to the new station's own beat from its first kicks, the tempo readout folds a half-time or double-time lock back to the tempo the song is really in, and the beat flash runs on real time, so a kick flashes for the same length on a 120 Hz phone as on a 60 Hz laptop. If a stream ever won't share its sound on your device, Beat Sync (a chip on the stage says when it's on) keeps every visualizer, the beat meters, and Auto-DJ dancing in time instead, and hands back to the real sound the moment that is flowing again. The same Beat Sync covers your music files too, and the song or station name up on the stage bounces along with the beat. Buffering is watched for, not waited on — on all seven stations alike, and now at the same fast pace for every one of them: while a station is meant to be playing, the player checks every second that the stream is really still flowing — the sound buffered in the range the music is actually playing from, not just any data the browser has tucked away — and the watch keeps running even while you browse the Music Player or Pride Updates views. Once a station is playing, if it says it is waiting for data and the sound doesn't return within about two seconds, if its playing buffer runs dry on three checks in a row, if the sound freezes for about three seconds, or if it keeps stuttering across a twelve-second stretch, it rejoins the live edge on a fresh connection automatically — for the five Amperwave stations (Q97.9, 92 Moose, Coast 93.1, The Wolf, and Frank FM) every rejoin asks their stream service for a brand-new session, which lands on a fresh streaming server. The wait between retries stays short for every station now — it never grows beyond about three seconds — and the burst of stall signals one hiccup sends is treated as the single drop it is, so the player never stacks competing reconnects or inflates its retry count. If your device goes offline, the player stops spending retries on a dead connection and simply holds the station; the moment your connection comes back online it rejoins right away, with a fresh count, instead of waiting for the next retry — and when you return to the app from the background, a stream that kept playing is left alone while a frozen one is picked up within about a second rather than after a whole new grace period. Resuming after a long pause rejoins at the live edge too, instead of trusting a connection that went stale while the radio sat paused. A station that keeps flapping — a few seconds of sound, then buffering again — no longer resets its retry count on every brief burst of sound: the count only clears after twenty seconds of solid, healthy play, so a feed that won't settle escalates to a faster fresh rejoin, and to its other official feed where the station publishes one, instead of buffering on and off forever. That fast rescue runs on every device — Android phones and tablets, iPhone and iPad, Windows PCs, laptops and MacBooks, Chromebooks, and Linux: GayFM's feed — re-checked live on 2026-11-13 — hands out about sixteen seconds of music in a burst when a connection opens, then trickles at barely above the speed the music plays, with no HLS or lower-bitrate version published to switch to, and iPhones keep only a few seconds of a live stream buffered, so a small dip empties an iPhone's buffer first — but a longer dip starves any device's buffer the same way, which is why the two-second rescue that used to be GayFM's alone now covers all seven stations, and every rejoin opens a fresh connection and earns that opening burst again. Beat Sync keeps the visualizers flowing with the radio while it rejoins. Star a station to keep it in your favorites, use ⏮ / ⏭ in the transport to hop stations, and the sleep timer in Player Options stops the radio too.

🏳️‍🌈 About Gay Pride

Pride Month is celebrated every June, honoring the LGBTQ+ community and the Stonewall uprising of June 1969 in New York City — a turning point in the movement for equal rights. Pride is about visibility, dignity, and the simple, powerful idea that everyone deserves to live and love openly as who they are.

The rainbow flag, created by artist Gilbert Baker in 1978, flies as a symbol of diversity and hope — you'll see its colors flowing through every visualizer, latte, and page corner here. At this cafe, Pride isn't just a month: it's every day, in every song. Happy Pride! 🌈

📲 Install this app

Install the player on your device so it opens like a real app — full screen, with its own icon on your home screen or dock. It works on Android phones & tablets, iPhone & iPad, Windows PCs, Mac & MacBook, Chromebooks, and Linux — no account and no app-store download needed.

No install window appeared? Install it from your browser menu
  1. Android — Chrome: tap ⋮ (top right) → Install app or Add to Home screen, then tap Install.
  2. Android — Edge / Firefox / Samsung Internet: open the browser menu → Add to Home screen / Install.
  3. iPhone & iPad — Safari: tap the Share button (square with an arrow) → Add to Home Screen → Add. (Apple only allows this from Safari.)
  4. iPhone & iPad — Chrome: tap the Share icon in the address bar → Add to Home Screen → Add.
  5. Windows PC — Chrome or Edge: click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or the browser menu → Install Gay Pride Month Cafe Music Player… / Apps → Install this site as an app.
  6. Mac & MacBook — Chrome or Edge: click the install icon in the address bar, or the browser menu → Install… — the app lands in your Applications / Launchpad.
  7. Mac & MacBook — Safari: open File → Add to Dock… (macOS Sonoma or newer), name it, and click Add.
  8. Chromebook — Chrome: click the install icon in the address bar, or ⋮ → Install app.
  9. Firefox on a computer: Firefox itself does not install web apps — use Chrome, Edge, or Safari on the same device instead, or just keep using it right here in Firefox; everything still plays.
